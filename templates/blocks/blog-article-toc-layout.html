Systems practice

A design system becomes real at the moment of decision.

Components matter, but the durable value lives in the shared reasoning that surrounds them.

On this page
  1. Start with shared decisions
  2. Keep evidence close
  3. Design for maintenance

Teams rarely struggle because they have no artifacts. They struggle because the relationship between a decision, its evidence, and the people responsible for revisiting it is difficult to see.

Start with shared decisions

A useful system records enough context for another person to understand why a pattern exists, where it applies, and what would justify changing it. That context should be concise, current, and close to the implementation.

Keep evidence close

Examples, accessibility findings, and product outcomes give a rule meaning. When evidence lives somewhere else, the rule eventually becomes a slogan instead of a working constraint.

Clarity is not the absence of complexity. It is a dependable way through it.

Design for maintenance

Every pattern needs a visible owner, a review path, and a signal that tells the team when the current answer no longer serves the work. Maintenance is part of the interface between the system and its users.
